# Helping low-income first-time buyers: options and what the app could do

Confidence labels: **Certain** (well established), **Likely** (true in general, check details), **Verify** (amounts, windows and status change; confirm with the program before using).
Not legal or lending advice.

## The core problem in numbers (from the app's sample)
- Lower-income family of 4 in Orange County can pay about $2,426/month (statute formula, 2026 State table).
- That buys about $243,000 at 7.18%. A modest new home costs about $585,000 to create. Gap: about $342,000 per home.
- Down-payment help alone (usually $10,000 to $150,000) does not close a gap that size. The big levers lower the **price** (land, subsidy, shared equity) or the **monthly cost** (rate, HOA, taxes).

## 1. Lower the price the family has to finance (biggest impact)
| Option | How it helps | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Community land trust (CLT)** | Nonprofit keeps the land; family buys only the house. Takes land cost out of the price. Resale is capped so the home stays affordable for the next family. | Certain (model). Verify which CLTs operate in OC. |
| **Shared appreciation / shared equity loans** | A program pays part of the price; the family repays it plus a share of future appreciation when they sell or refinance. Example: CalHFA Dream For All (up to 20%, capped, first-generation buyers, lottery windows). | Likely. Verify the current round and cap. |
| **Silent second loans** (city, county, state HOME, CalHome) | Deferred loans with no monthly payment, repaid on sale or refinance; some are forgivable. Stack with each other. | Certain (type). Verify amounts per city. |
| **Inclusionary / density-bonus units** | Cities require builders to sell some units below market. Prices are set by the affordability formula. | Certain (exists). Verify each city's list. |
| **Sweat equity** | Family puts in build hours instead of part of the down payment. Lowers cash needed, builds skills. | Certain (model). |
| **Donated or surplus public land** | Church land, school district or city surplus land at low or no cost. | Likely. Verify per site (state surplus land and faith-based housing laws). |

## 2. Co-ownership and shared models (your "co-owner" idea)
| Option | How it works | Watch out |
|---|---|---|
| **Co-buying with family or friends** | Two households share one loan and title (often tenancy in common) with a written co-ownership agreement. | Both incomes count for qualifying, and both are on the hook. Needs an exit plan for when one wants to sell. |
| **Limited-equity housing co-op** | Residents own shares in a co-op that owns the building; share price rises slowly by formula. | Fewer lenders finance co-op shares. |
| **Lease-to-own / rent-to-own run by a nonprofit** | Family rents, part of rent saved toward a down payment, buys after 2 to 5 years. | Avoid predatory private versions. A nonprofit or CLT version is safer. |
| **ADU or duplex "house hack"** | Family buys a home with a second unit; rent from the unit helps pay the mortgage. | Some lenders count only part of the rent. |
| **Employer co-investment** | Employer gives a forgivable loan or matched savings, or builds workforce homes. | Usually tied to staying employed. |

## 3. Lower the monthly cost (keeps the family in the home)
- **Rate buydowns** funded by a seller, builder or program. In the app, every 0.25% is worth about $4,800 of gap per home. **Likely**
- **Below-market first mortgages** from state housing finance agencies or community banks under the Community Reinvestment Act. **Likely**
- **Special Purpose Credit Programs** (some banks offer grants or better terms for underserved buyers). **Verify** per bank.
- **Smaller, simpler homes** with low HOA dues. HOA dues come straight out of what the family can borrow ($425/month removes about $52,000 of buying power in the sample). **Certain** (math).
- **Property tax welfare exemption** for CLT and nonprofit homes in California, which lowers taxes. **Likely** (verify eligibility).

## 4. Get the family ready (cheap, high impact)
- **HUD-approved housing counseling** and first-time buyer education. Many programs require it. **Certain**
- **Matched savings accounts (IDAs)**: each $1 saved is matched $1 to $3. **Likely** (verify local providers).
- **Credit building**: report rent payments, pay down "pay-later" debt (the Herreras' $297.50/month). **Certain**
- **Document readiness**: pay stubs, bank statements, letters for large deposits. The app's document reader already flags these. **Certain**

## 5. Protect the family after purchase
- Escrow for taxes and insurance; warn about **supplemental tax bills** and **insurance nonrenewals** (starter kit docs 05 to 07).
- Emergency repair funds and foreclosure-prevention counseling.
- Resale help inside CLT or shared-equity programs.

## What the app could add next (ranked by demo value vs effort)
1. **Funding stack builder** (small): a picklist of program *types* (CLT land write-down, silent second, shared appreciation, employer, donor, rate buydown) with how each moves the gap. Amounts stay as inputs with sources.
2. **Co-buyer toggle** (small): add a second household's income; show the new affordable price, and flag that both are on the loan.
3. **CLT mode** (small): remove land cost from the price; show the resale cap.
4. **Rate buydown slider** (small): "a $X buydown lowers the gap by $Y" using the existing rate math.
5. **Program matcher** (large): match a household to programs. Needs a maintained, dated program list, so mark anything not confirmed.
